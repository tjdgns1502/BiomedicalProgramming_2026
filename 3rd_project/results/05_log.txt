
== Counterexample A: minimal construction ==
X = 0.5*U(-1,0) + 0.5*U(0,10);  Y = 0.5*U(-10,0) + 0.5*U(0,1)
exact P(X<Y) = 0.25*(0.05 + 1 + 0 + 0.05) = 0.275

simulation with 4e+06 draws:
median_X median_Y   mean_X   mean_Y   p_less    cliff 
 0.00314 -0.00544  2.25014 -2.25069  0.27460  0.45044 

|P(X<Y) simulated - exact| = 0.000401   (MC SE = 0.000223)
Cliff's delta: exact 0.45, simulated 0.45044

rejection rates at n = 20 per group (B = 10000):
  welch student     wmw 
   99.7    99.7    71.9 
  guide's stated WMW value: ~71%

-- the weakness of Counterexample A, stated plainly --
  means differ: 2.25 vs -2.25 (difference 4.5)
  so Welch rejects 99.7% of the time too. A proves 'WMW is not a median test';
  it does NOT prove the paradox, because the t-test is not wrong here.


== Counterexample B: paper-style construction ==
X: lognormal(meanlog = 0, sdlog = 0.71557)
Y: -0.46196 + lognormal(meanlog = 0.37978, sdlog = 0.60328)

                    X          Y
mean         1.29178    1.29178   (equal: TRUE)
median       1.00000    1.00000   (equal: TRUE)
sd           1.05633    1.16196   (ratio: 1.10000)

Marginal rejection rates agree (both are unbiased). What breaks is the
PAIRED quantity P(p_WMW < p_Welch): with independent samples it is
estimated with much larger variance and loses its meaning as a
within-sample comparison, which is exactly what the paper's Table 5 is.

SD of the estimated P(p_WMW < p_Welch) across 40 independent runs of
B = 1,000 at n = 250:
  common random numbers : 1.228 pp
  independent samples   : 1.517 pp
  variance ratio        : 1.52x

-- how to read this --
WMW's rising rejection rate is POWER, not Type I error: its own null
hypothesis P(X<Y) = 0.5 is false here (P = 0.4825).
The error is in translating that p-value into 'the medians differ',
when the medians are identical by construction.
