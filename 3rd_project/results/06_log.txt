The raw-mean-difference permutation test tracks Student's t, not
Welch's: it inflates when the small group has the larger SD and turns
conservative when the large group does. Studentizing repairs it.


== (2) rank permutation test == WMW, by complete enumeration ==
permutations enumerated per replicate: 1716  (C(13,6))
max |p_permutation - p_wilcox(exact)| over 25 replicates: 5.55e-17
identical to machine precision: TRUE

So WMW's null really is 'the labels are exchangeable', which is why it
reacts to a difference in spread and not only to a difference in
location. That is the whole mechanism behind the paper.

Var(d) = s1^2 + s2^2 - 2*rho*s1*s2 is confirmed; positive rho shrinks
it, which is where the paired design's extra power comes from.

Naming: the PAIRED rank test is Wilcoxon's SIGNED-RANK test; the
independent-samples one is the RANK-SUM test. Same R function,
different statistic, different null.
